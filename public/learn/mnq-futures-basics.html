MNQ futures basics

The Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 contract in plain numbers — what a point is worth, when it trades, and what each trade really costs.

The contract in one table

UnderlyingNasdaq-100 index (NQ's little sibling, 1/10th the size)
Point value$2 per index point per contract
Tick size0.25 points = $0.50 per contract
HoursNearly 24/5, Sunday evening to Friday afternoon ET, with a daily maintenance halt
Regular session (RTH)9:30–16:00 ET — where most volume and most setups live

The arithmetic you will use constantly: a 10-point stop is $20 of risk per contract; a 40-point move is $80 per contract. Multiply by your contract count and the numbers stay honest.

Session structure matters more than the clock

Overnight (Globex) trade is thinner and tends to drift; the regular session brings the volume, the range, and the reactions at prior-day levels. Most intraday traders build their plan from the overnight high and low, prior-day high and low, and prior close — then trade how price behaves at those references after the open. If you learn one habit, learn to mark those levels before 9:30.

What a trade actually costs

Two costs hit every round trip, and simulators that skip them flatter you:

Commissions and fees — typically about $0.75–$1.50 per side per contract all-in at retail futures brokers, so roughly $1.50–$3.00 per round trip.

Slippage and spread — market orders in MNQ commonly fill a tick against you, and stops are market orders once triggered. Call it another $1 per round trip per contract in normal conditions, worse in fast tape.

Say $4 per round trip per contract, all-in. Trade 10 round trips a day and you start every session about $40 per contract behind. A strategy that scalps 2–3 points per trade lives or dies on exactly this line item — which is why practicing without costs teaches a strategy that does not exist.

Why train on MNQ specifically

It moves enough to teach real reading — trends, traps, failed breakouts — while the micro sizing keeps the dollar swings survivable for evaluation-scale accounts. The habits transfer directly to NQ later; only the multiplier changes.
