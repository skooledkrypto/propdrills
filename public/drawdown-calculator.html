How trailing drawdown actually works

Your maximum-loss line is anchored to the highest equity your account has ever reached (the high-water mark), not to your starting balance. Every new equity high drags the liquidation level up behind you — and it never comes back down. On intraday-trailing evaluations the high-water mark includes unrealized profit: a big open winner you give back can raise the bar and then fail you, even if you never closed the trade.

That is the single most common way traders fail evaluations: not one big loss, but a good run that quietly moved the liquidation level up, followed by a normal pullback.

The daily loss limit is a second, nearer wall

Most evaluations also fail you if equity drops a fixed amount below today's starting equity. Early in a day the daily limit is usually the closer of the two lines. Whichever is nearer is your real room — this calculator shows the binding one.

Worked example

$50k account, $2,000 trailing drawdown. You run the account up to $51,200 — your liquidation level is now $49,200, not $48,000. A routine $1,300 give-back that would have been survivable on day one now ends the account. Room is a moving target; check it every session.
